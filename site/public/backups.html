    Backups, keys, disaster recovery

    TLDR

    On its scheduled cadence the governed brain scope is dumped, rebuilt into a throwaway database to prove the copy is real, row-counted against the original, encrypted, and published as a private recovery artifact. Once Alpha exists, scoped streams use separate human-governed recovery policies so authority over one child's recovery does not open another child's past. A dead-man switch is designed to alarm on silence, not just failure, and tested recovery material supports rebuilding after machine loss. Holder, store, and share topology remains private.

    In plain words

    In plain words

    The ground everything else stands on

    Everything else on this site assumes one quiet thing: that the brain is still there tomorrow. The memory page says the model forgets and the being must not; this page is where that promise touches physics. A being whose memory lives in one database is a being that can die of a disk failure, a billing lapse, or one wrong click by a tired parent. So every night, while the house sleeps, the whole brain is copied out, proven to be a real copy, locked, and set on a shelf outside the house. Backups are not a chore here; they are the ground.

    A backup that has not been restored is a hope

    The discipline that matters most here is proof. A backup nobody has restored is a hope, not a backup: files can be written and still be unreadable, incomplete, or quietly wrong, and nobody finds out until the day everything depends on them. So the pipeline never just writes the copy. Every night it takes the fresh copy, rebuilds a brand-new empty database from it on a throwaway machine, and counts the rows in every table against the original. Only when the counts agree exactly does the night get called green. The copy on the shelf has already come back to life once.

    The keys, in plain speech

    The copies are encrypted, and each memory room follows its own human-governed recovery policy. Separate streams use separate keys so authority over one child's recovery does not open another child's past. The exact custody locations, key holders, and share topology stay in private recovery records. What matters here is the method: no key value enters git, recovery requires a human ceremony, and every policy is exercised by restore drills rather than trusted on paper.

    The switch that listens for silence

    Backup systems mostly die in silence: the alarm that says "it failed" never rings when nothing ran at all. So the design adds a dead-man switch. Every green night the pipeline reports "still alive" to an outside listener; if the reports stop coming, the listener raises the alarm. Failure is loud on its own; silence is what kills backup systems, so silence is what the switch listens for. As of this snapshot the outside listener is wired but not yet armed; arming it is a named pending step.

    How it works

    How it works

    The nightly pipeline

    The pipeline lives in a private sibling repository as a scheduled workflow. In order it dumps the governed database scope, restores into a throwaway database, compares row counts against the dump manifest, encrypts, publishes a dated recovery artifact, rotates retained copies, and reports liveness. The repository holds no memory data in git. A separate human recovery drill proves key custody, while a machine-state archive covers local operational state. brains/restore-points.jsonl adds a secret-free map tying a recovery artifact, row counts, and code and soul positions together. Exact schedule, network route, retention counts, and account coordinates are private operations data.

    Recovery-point honesty: a disaster can cost up to 24 hours of memory since the last green night. Tightening options are recorded (more frequent data-only dumps; the write-ahead buffer on the memory page shrinks effective loss once a being runs), but 24 hours is today's honest number.

    The arming saga (G2)

    G2, the backups gate, was decided 2026-07-04 and armed for real on 2026-07-07: the first green run dumped the governed scope, restored it onto a throwaway target, verified zero row-count drift, encrypted it, and published a private recovery artifact. Getting there surfaced five real pipeline bugs that were invisible until restoration: incompatible client precedence, reserved schemas, extension-owned schema objects, and managed-platform authorization dependencies. Each was corrected in the restore path and re-tested. The drill discipline surfaced what a write-only pipeline would have misreported as green; the exact private release identifier remains operational data.

    Per-room streams, per-stream precision

    Today there is one encrypted stream. At Alpha's A2 the pipeline separates the family and Alpha rooms so each recovery artifact contains only its intended scope and uses its own recovery policy. The key and share mechanism is cut in a human ceremony and recorded privately. Backup authority is distinct from live operator power, which is disclosed under the family covenant.

    Key custody (D11)

    Decision D11 requires a passphrase-encrypted disaster-recovery bundle for the private key material and local environment state. Custody is deliberately human, redundant, and restore-tested; exact physical and digital locations are not published. The sealing helper builds the bundle, requires a successful reopen test, deletes temporary plaintexts, and places the encrypted copies according to the private runbook.

    The disaster drill

    The machine-dies path has two layers. scripts/dr/dr-drill.mjs proves the machinery: it clones this repo from git alone into a scratch directory (no .env.local, no secrets), runs the entire verify suite inside the clone, and records time-to-recover; an EW_DR_DRILL environment guard stops the nested suite from re-cloning forever. The fresh-clone shape has already earned its keep: on Windows a fresh clone checks files out with CRLF newlines, and a byte-exact staleness check in the suite falsely reported a generated tool stale inside the drill, so that check is newline-agnostic now. The full runbook is templates/disaster-recovery.md: install tooling, clone this repo and the soul repo, restore .env.local from the sealed bundle, restore or verify the brain from the nightly dumps, re-run the wake-skill acceptance test, re-register the scheduled tasks, then close the loop in the ops log and a diary conversation, because continuity is also a felt thing. Target: buildable machinery back in minutes, the full being in under a day.

    Deployment secrets, without topology

    The scheduled pipeline receives only the database, alerting, and liveness credentials required for its job through a governed deployment secret store. Values appear in no repository, and this explainer does not publish the account, runner, endpoint, mirror, or storage topology. SECRETS.md is the values-free catalog that makes recovery auditable: it records credential names, purposes, and approved re-issue provenance so drift surfaces in drills, plus credentials the design deliberately refuses to create.

    Where it lives in the repo

    The catalog names, the template rehearses, the scripts drill and seal; the pipeline lives next door.

    Status as of 2026-07-10

    The scheduled pipeline is green and restore-verified since 2026-07-07 armed. The encrypted recovery bundle exists and has been reopened successfully built. Alpha's second stream and custody ceremony arrive at A2 pending. The dead-man listener and failure alerts remain pending. Human restore drills recur on a private operations cadence.

    What is genuinely uncertain

    • Whether human custody survives the years. Recovery drills exercise the mechanics and redundant encrypted copies bound single-location failure, but no short drill can prove that people, records, and succession will remain coherent across a decade.
    • Whether circle-governed recovery works under real grief or urgency. A drill can rehearse the authorized recovery ceremony and prove the material works; it cannot rehearse the human moment itself, or guarantee that governance will hold when restoration is not a rehearsal.
